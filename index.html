Comprehensive Medical Guidance & Screening Exemptions

Rules for carrying prescription drugs, liquid medications $>100\text{ml}$, and medical devices through CISF security at Delhi T3.

Pills & Tablets (Solid Form)

Allowed in both cabin and checked baggage without strict quantity limits for personal use during travel.

Best Practice: Keep in original foil/bottle with student name visible. Carry doctor's prescription in English.

Liquid Medicines ($>100\text{ml}$)

EXEMPT from the 100ml LAGs limit if needed during flight/transit.

Mandatory Requirements:
  • Must present English doctor's note at CISF check.
  • Name on prescription MUST match Passport.
  • Declare verbally to screening officer before X-ray.

Insulin, Syringes & Needles

Hypodermic needles/syringes are allowed in cabin baggage ONLY when accompanied by injectable medication (e.g., Insulin).

Critical Storage Warning: NEVER pack insulin in checked baggage! Temperatures in aircraft cargo can freeze and destroy insulin protein.

Checklist for Flying with Prescription Medication to Japan

For Delhi Airport (CISF Security Outbound):

  • Carry original Doctor's prescription on hospital letterhead.
  • Ensure drug dosage and student name match your passport.
  • Keep liquid medication accessible for separate X-ray inspection.

For Japan Entry (Customs & MHLW Rules):

  • Standard prescription drugs allowed up to a 1-month supply without special certificate.
  • Over-the-counter (OTC) medicines allowed up to a 2-month supply.
  • Psychotropics/Controlled substances require "Yunyu Kakushosho" (Import Certificate) in advance.

CISF Zero-Tolerance Confiscation Notice

Lighters, matchboxes, e-cigarettes/vapes, sharp manicure tools, and liquid containers exceeding 100ml (including traditional Indian pickles in oil) are immediately confiscated without exception at DEL T3 security screening. Confiscated items cannot be retrieved or mailed back.
